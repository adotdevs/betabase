What is cryptocurrency?

A cryptocurrency is a digital asset whose ownership is recorded on a shared public ledger — a blockchain — rather than by a single bank or government.

The short version

Traditional money exists as an entry in your bank's private database. Your bank alone decides what that database says. A cryptocurrency instead records balances on a ledger that thousands of independent computers hold a copy of, and they agree on updates using cryptography and a set of shared rules. No single party can quietly rewrite it.

Key ideas

  • Decentralised. No central issuer or administrator for most major coins.
  • Scarce by design. Many coins have a fixed or predictable supply written into their code. Scarcity does not guarantee value.
  • Divisible. You can own a tiny fraction of a coin. You never have to buy a whole one.
  • Coins vs tokens. A coin is native to its own blockchain. A token is issued on top of someone else's blockchain.
  • Pseudonymous, not anonymous. Transactions are public; addresses are not directly linked to names, but they can often be traced.

Good to know

  • Crypto is not legal tender in most countries, is not covered by deposit-guarantee schemes, and carries no interest by default.
  • Prices are set purely by supply and demand and can move a long way in a single day.
  • Crypto is volatile and your capital is at risk. This article is educational and is not investment advice.
