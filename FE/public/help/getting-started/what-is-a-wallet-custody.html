What is a wallet, and who holds my coins?

A wallet stores the private keys that let you move crypto. On Betabase your balances are custodial — Betabase holds the keys on your behalf.

The two models

  • Custodial (what you have here). Betabase safeguards the private keys. You sign in with an email, password and 2FA — the familiar model. If you forget your password you can reset it. You are trusting Betabase to hold the assets properly.
  • Self-custody (an external wallet). You hold the private keys yourself, backed up by a seed phrase (usually 12 or 24 words). Nobody can freeze or seize the wallet — and nobody can help you if you lose the phrase. Lost seed phrase means lost funds, permanently.

Good to know

  • You can withdraw from Betabase to a self-custody wallet at any time, subject to your limits and network availability.
  • Your Betabase password is not a seed phrase. Betabase does not issue you a seed phrase for your custodial balance.
  • Crypto held on any platform is generally not protected by deposit-guarantee or investor-compensation schemes the way bank cash is. Check the Terms of Service for how assets are held.
  • A seed phrase is a master key. Anyone who reads it owns the funds.

Betabase will never ask for your password, 2FA code, seed phrase or private keys. Never type a seed phrase into any website, form, chat or "wallet validation" tool.
