Do I have to pay tax on crypto?

Possibly — but tax treatment depends entirely on your personal circumstances and the country you are tax-resident in, and only a qualified tax adviser can tell you what you owe.

This article is general information, not tax advice. Betabase does not provide tax advice and cannot tell you what you owe, at what rate, or on which transactions. Rules differ between countries, change frequently, and depend on facts specific to you. Consult a qualified tax adviser or your national tax authority.

Events that are commonly treated as taxable somewhere

This is a list of possibilities to discuss with your adviser — not a statement of what applies to you:

  • Selling crypto for cash.
  • Swapping one crypto asset for another (often treated as a disposal even though no cash was received).
  • Spending crypto on goods or services.
  • Receiving crypto as income — earnings, staking or similar rewards, referral bonuses, airdrops.
  • Gifting crypto, in some jurisdictions.

Events commonly treated as not taxable in many jurisdictions

  • Buying crypto with cash and simply holding it.
  • Moving your own crypto between your own wallets.

Again: whether these hold for you depends on your jurisdiction.

What you should do

  1. Keep complete records. Export your full transaction history (see How to download a tax / cost report) and retain it for as long as your jurisdiction requires.
  2. Give the export to a qualified tax adviser, or to tax software your adviser recommends.
  3. Check your national tax authority's own published guidance on crypto assets.

Good to know

  • We may be legally required to report certain account and transaction information to tax authorities. That does not calculate or settle your liability — that remains your responsibility.
  • We will never state a tax rate, confirm your liability, or tell you a transaction is tax-free.
