How to take a photo of your ID that passes first time

Most verification failures are photo quality, not identity problems — flat lighting, a dark background, no glare and all four corners in frame will fix nearly all of them.

Steps

  1. Take the document out of any wallet, sleeve or plastic holder. Even a clear sleeve creates reflections.
  2. Lay it flat on a dark, plain, matte surface. A white table makes the edges impossible to detect.
  3. Use indirect daylight. Turn off the flash and step away from overhead spotlights, which bounce off the laminate.
  4. Hold the phone directly above the document, parallel to it — not at an angle.
  5. Fill the frame, but leave a small margin so all four corners are clearly inside it.
  6. Wait for the camera to focus before you press the button. Tap the text to force focus if needed.
  7. Check the photo yourself before submitting: can you read every character of the name, date of birth and document number?

Good to know

  • Glare is the number one cause of rejection. If a white patch covers any text, retake it.
  • Blur usually means you moved. Rest your elbows on the table.
  • Do not crop, rotate, filter, brighten or edit the image in any way. Any edit makes the document look tampered with and it will be rejected.
  • Never photograph a document displayed on a screen, and never send a screenshot.
  • For the selfie: remove hats, sunglasses and face coverings, face a light source, and use a plain background.

Only upload documents through the verification flow inside your logged-in account. Never email or message a photo of your ID to anyone claiming to be from Betabase.
