Verifying a self-hosted (private) wallet you send to

Before you withdraw to a self-hosted (private) wallet, regulation may require you to prove that the wallet is yours — usually by signing a message or receiving a tiny test transfer.

Why we ask

A self-hosted wallet has no exchange behind it, so there is no institution we can ask who owns it. Rules such as the Travel Rule require us to know whether a receiving wallet belongs to you or to a third party. Confirming ownership lets us release larger withdrawals without a manual review each time.

Steps

  1. Go to Withdraw → Crypto and add the destination address.
  2. When prompted, declare whether the wallet is yours or belongs to someone else. Answer honestly — a false declaration breaches the Terms of Service.
  3. If it is yours, choose a proof method:

- Message signing. We show a short message. Paste it into your wallet's Sign message function, then paste the resulting signature back into Betabase. This proves you control the private key and costs nothing. - Micro-transfer ("Satoshi test"). We send a tiny amount and ask you to confirm the exact value received, or you send a small amount from that wallet to us. - Screenshot verification. For wallets that cannot sign, we may accept a screenshot of the wallet app showing the address and your ownership. This is the weakest method and is not available everywhere.

  1. Once confirmed, the address is marked Verified and stays in your address book.

Good to know

  • Hardware wallets (Ledger, Trezor and similar) support message signing through their companion apps.
  • Not every wallet supports signing. If yours does not, the flow offers you an alternative.
  • Verification is per address, not per wallet app.

Betabase will never ask for your seed phrase or private keys. Signing a message never requires either — if any "verification tool" asks for your 12 or 24 words, it is theft. On-chain transfers cannot be reversed.
