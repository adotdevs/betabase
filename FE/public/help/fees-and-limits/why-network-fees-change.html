Why network fees change (gas / congestion)

Network fees change because block space is scarce and priced by demand. When more people want their transaction in the next block than there is room for, they bid the fee up.

How it works

  1. A blockchain fits only a limited amount of data into each block, and blocks arrive at a fixed rate.
  2. Pending transactions wait in the mempool. Validators pick the most profitable ones first.
  3. To go first, you attach a higher fee. This bidding is what "gas" prices on Ethereum-style chains describe.
  4. When demand falls, so does the price — which is why the same withdrawal can cost a fraction of yesterday's fee.

What drives spikes

  • A market-wide surge in trading, a popular launch, or a liquidation cascade.
  • Bots competing for the same block.
  • Complex transactions consuming more block space than a simple transfer.

How to pay less

  • Withdraw once for a larger amount rather than several times for small ones — the fee is per transaction, not per coin.
  • Where the asset supports more than one network, compare the fee for each before you choose. But only pick a network your destination wallet or exchange actually supports.
  • Send at quieter times. Fees are often lower outside peak trading hours.

Good to know

  • Betabase passes the network fee through and does not profit from it.
  • The fee estimate shown is exactly that — an estimate at the moment you look.

Choosing the wrong network to save on fees is the most expensive mistake in crypto. Funds sent over an unsupported network are usually unrecoverable.
