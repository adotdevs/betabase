Withdrawal and network fees explained

Withdrawing EUR to your bank may carry a flat fee. Withdrawing crypto carries a network fee, which is paid to the blockchain's validators — Betabase does not keep it.

EUR withdrawals

  • A flat fee may apply per withdrawal, depending on your country and method. The amount is shown on the withdrawal screen.
  • Funds go only to a bank account in your own name.
  • Standard transfers take 1–2 business days. Instant methods, where available, are faster and may cost more.

Crypto withdrawals

  • The network fee is what the blockchain charges to include your transaction in a block. It rises when the network is busy and falls when it is quiet.
  • The fee is quoted in the asset being sent (or in that chain's native coin) and is deducted from the amount withdrawn or charged on top — the summary shows which.
  • The fee is the same whether you send a large amount or a small one. Many small withdrawals therefore cost far more in total than one large one.
  • Different networks cost very different amounts. Where an asset exists on more than one network, the withdrawal screen shows the fee for each.

Good to know

  • Betabase cannot refund a network fee. Once broadcast, it is spent — even if you sent to the wrong address.
  • A low fee at a busy moment can leave a transaction unconfirmed for a long time.

On-chain transfers are irreversible. Check the address and the network before confirming. Sending on the wrong network usually means permanent loss.
