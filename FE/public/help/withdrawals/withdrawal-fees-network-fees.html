Withdrawal fees and network fees explained

There are two separate costs: a withdrawal fee charged by Betabase, and a network fee charged by the blockchain itself — Betabase does not set or keep the network fee.

The two fees

  • Platform withdrawal fee. Set by Betabase and published under Fees & limits. It may be a flat amount or a percentage, and it can differ between fiat and crypto.
  • Network fee (gas / miner fee). Paid to the validators who process your transaction. It is set by supply and demand on the network at that moment — not by Betabase — and it can swing dramatically within a single hour when the network is congested.

For fiat, your own bank and any intermediary bank may also deduct a fee, especially on international wires.

Good to know

  • The total cost, and the net amount that will actually arrive, is always shown before you confirm — never after.
  • If you select Max, the fees are deducted from the amount sent, so slightly less arrives than your headline balance.
  • Choosing a cheaper network for the same asset (where both sides support it) is usually the single biggest saving available.
  • Withdrawing once for a larger amount costs less in total fees than several small withdrawals.
  • A network fee is not refundable. It is paid even if the receiving wallet turns out to be wrong.

Never send a withdrawal that would leave less than the receiving side's minimum after fees — the funds may be unusable. Blockchain transactions are irreversible.
