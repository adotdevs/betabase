Trading pairs and what they mean

A trading pair names the two assets in a trade: the base asset you are buying or selling, and the quote asset it is priced in — written base/quote, such as BTC/EUR.

How to read one

  • In BTC/EUR, BTC is the base and EUR is the quote. A price of 50,000 means one BTC costs 50,000 EUR.
  • Buying the pair means buying the base with the quote. Selling the pair means the reverse.
  • In a crypto-to-crypto pair such as ETH/BTC, the price is expressed in BTC, not in EUR. Your EUR value moves with both assets.

Good to know

  • Not every asset trades against every other. Availability depends on liquidity, licensing in your country and the asset's listing status.
  • If the pair you want isn't offered, route through EUR or a major asset in two orders. You pay the spread twice.
  • Each pair has its own minimum trade amount, its own spread and its own price precision (the number of decimals).
  • A pair may be suspended temporarily — during a network upgrade, a listing review or extreme volatility. Suspended pairs cannot be traded in either direction until they reopen.
  • Prices for the same asset can differ slightly between pairs. That is normal and reflects each pair's own supply and demand.
