Understanding deposit addresses and networks (choosing the right chain)

A deposit address only works for the specific asset and the specific network it was generated for — choosing the wrong network is the most common way people lose crypto.

Why networks matter

The same token often exists on several blockchains at once. USDT, for example, runs on Ethereum (ERC-20), Tron (TRC-20), BNB Chain (BEP-20), Solana and others. They share a name and a price, but they are separate assets on separate ledgers that cannot see each other. An ERC-20 address and a TRC-20 address are not interchangeable, even when they look similar.

How to choose

  1. In Betabase, open Deposit → Crypto and select your asset. The supported networks are listed.
  2. Open your sending wallet or exchange and look at which networks it offers for the withdrawal.
  3. Pick a network that appears on both lists.
  4. Set that network on the sending side before you paste the address.
  5. Compare the first four and last four characters of the pasted address against the original. Address-swapping malware exists.
  6. Send a small test amount first, wait for it to arrive, then send the rest.

Good to know

  • Networks differ in cost and speed. Ethereum is often the most expensive; alternatives are usually cheaper and faster. If both sides support several, the cheaper one is fine.
  • Ethereum-style addresses (starting 0x) look identical across Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon and others — the address format tells you nothing about which chain is correct.
  • Layer-2 networks (for example Arbitrum, Optimism, Base) are separate networks too, even though they use 0x addresses.

Blockchain transactions are irreversible. Sending on an unsupported network usually means permanent loss, and there is no undo — not by you, not by Betabase, not by anyone.
