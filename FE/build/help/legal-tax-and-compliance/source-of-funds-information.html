Why we ask for source-of-funds information

We are legally required to understand where the money you deposit comes from — it is a standard anti-money-laundering obligation, not a judgement about you.

Why it happens

Regulated firms must know their customers and must be able to explain the origin of the funds passing through them. A source-of-funds request is usually triggered by routine, risk-based rules — for example a first large deposit, a pattern that differs from your stated activity, or a periodic review. Everyone gets asked eventually.

What we may ask for

  • Employment income: recent payslips, an employment contract, or bank statements showing the salary credit.
  • Self-employment or business income: accounts, tax returns, or business bank statements.
  • Sale of an asset: a completion statement, a sale contract, or a solicitor's letter.
  • Inheritance or gift: a grant of probate, a solicitor's letter, or a signed letter from the giver plus proof of their source.
  • Savings accumulated over time: statements showing the build-up.
  • Existing crypto holdings: exchange statements or on-chain history showing where the assets came from.

Steps

  1. Open the request in Profile → Verification and read exactly which document type is asked for.
  2. Upload clear, complete, unedited documents — all pages, showing your name and the dates.
  3. Submit and wait. You'll be notified when the review completes.

Good to know

  • Your account may be restricted to withdrawal-only while a request is open. Responding promptly is the fastest route back to normal.
  • We cannot always explain in detail why a specific request was raised — in some jurisdictions the law prohibits it.
  • Documents are handled under our privacy policy and retained only as long as the law requires.
