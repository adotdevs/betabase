Trading fees explained (spread vs commission)

There are two ways a trade can cost you money: a spread hidden inside the price, and a commission shown as a separate line. Some markets use one, some use both.

Spread

The spread is the gap between the price you can buy at and the price you can sell at. It is not a line item — it is baked into the quote. It explains why an asset bought and instantly sold shows a small loss even with zero commission. Spreads are wider on low-liquidity assets, at weekends and during volatile news.

Commission

A commission is an explicit percentage of the order value, shown separately in the summary and on your transaction record. Where a maker/taker model applies, an order that adds liquidity to the book (a resting limit order) may be charged less than one that takes it away (a market order).

Good to know

  • Only executed volume is charged. Cancelled and unfilled orders are free, and a partially filled order is charged only on the part that filled.
  • Fee tiers may reduce your rate as your 30-day volume rises. Your current tier is shown in Profile → Fees.
  • To judge the true cost of a trade, look at what you actually receive, not at the commission line alone. The summary screen gives you exactly that.

A low headline commission can hide a wide spread. Always compare the quantity you receive, not the advertised fee percentage.
