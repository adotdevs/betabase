How to spot phishing and fake support

Phishing tries to get you to hand over your password, 2FA code or crypto by pretending to be Betabase. The single best defence is to never act on an incoming message.

The rules that keep you safe

  • Betabase will never ask for your password, 2FA code, seed phrase or private keys. No exceptions, no emergencies, no "verification checks".
  • We never ask you to install remote-access or screen-sharing software.
  • We never DM you first on Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord, X or Instagram. Every "support agent" who contacts you there is a fraudster.
  • We never ask you to move funds to a "safe wallet", "vault" or "recovery address". That is theft, described in reassuring language.
  • We never phone you to ask you to approve a withdrawal.

Red flags

  • Urgency and threats: "your account will be closed in 24 hours".
  • A link whose domain is nearly, but not exactly, www.betabase.pro — extra hyphens, a different ending, a lookalike character.
  • Unexpected attachments, or a login page reached from an email rather than typed by you.
  • Anyone offering to "recover" funds you already lost, for an upfront fee. That is a second scam targeting victims of the first.

What to do if you clicked

  1. Freeze your account immediately.
  2. Change your Betabase password and your email password.
  3. Reset 2FA and revoke unknown sessions and devices.
  4. Report it to support@www.betabase.pro with screenshots.

On-chain transfers cannot be reversed. If you send crypto to a scammer, Betabase cannot get it back.
