Why the price I got differs from the price I saw (slippage & spread)

The price you see is a live quote; the price you get is the price at the exact moment your order executes. The gap between them comes from spread, slippage and quote expiry.

The three causes

  • Spread. Every market has a buy price and a slightly lower sell price. The difference is the spread. It is a real cost, and it is why a coin you buy and instantly sell shows a small loss.
  • Slippage. Between you tapping Confirm and the order executing, the market moves. In volatile conditions or on thinly traded assets, that move can be meaningful. Larger orders can also consume several price levels, so the average fill price is worse than the top price.
  • Quote expiry. Quotes are valid for a few seconds. Let one lapse and the refreshed quote reflects a new market.

Good to know

  • Spread is widest on low-liquidity assets, at weekends and during sudden news.
  • The order summary shows the estimated price before you confirm. Nothing is added afterwards.
  • To control the price rather than the timing, use a limit order — it will not fill above (buy) or below (sell) your chosen level.
  • Slippage is a market effect, not a Betabase fee, and it can also work in your favour.

Betabase cannot reverse a filled order because the price moved. Crypto is volatile and your capital is at risk.
