Market orders vs limit orders explained

A market order prioritises speed and fills immediately at the best available price. A limit order prioritises price and fills only at the price you set, or better — if the market ever reaches it.

Market orders

  • Executes right away against the best available prices.
  • You control the amount, not the price. The final price can differ slightly from the quote you saw (see Why the price I got differs from the price I saw).
  • Best when you want certainty that the order will happen.

Limit orders

  • You set a price. A buy limit fills at your price or lower; a sell limit fills at your price or higher.
  • You control the price, not whether it fills. If the market never reaches your price, the order simply stays open — potentially forever.
  • Your cash (for a buy) or crypto (for a sell) is reserved while the order is open, so you cannot spend it elsewhere.
  • Large limit orders may fill in pieces (see Order status).

Good to know

  • A limit order set far from the current price can sit open for weeks. Set an expiry (Good til cancelled, Good for day) if your platform region offers one.
  • In a fast-moving market a limit order can be skipped entirely if the price gaps straight through your level.
  • Neither order type protects you from loss. Neither is "safer" than the other in the sense that matters — the asset's price can still fall.
