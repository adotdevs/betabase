Is crypto risky?

Yes. Crypto is a high-risk, highly volatile asset class. Prices can fall sharply and without warning, and you can lose some or all of the money you put in.

The main risks

  • Volatility. Double-digit percentage moves in a single day are normal, in both directions. There is no circuit breaker and the market never closes.
  • Total loss. An asset's value can go to zero. Projects fail, are abandoned, or turn out to be fraudulent. Past performance tells you nothing about the future.
  • Irreversibility. On-chain transfers cannot be reversed. A mistyped address or a scam payment is usually unrecoverable.
  • Liquidity. Smaller assets can be hard to sell at the quoted price during stress, and spreads widen.
  • Scams and social engineering. Fake support, fake "recovery" services, romance and investment scams, and cloned websites all target crypto users specifically.
  • Regulatory change. Rules differ by country and can change quickly, affecting availability and tax treatment.

How to reduce (not remove) risk

  • Only commit money you can genuinely afford to lose.
  • Take time to understand an asset before you buy it.
  • Turn on 2FA and use a unique password.
  • Be sceptical of anything promising guaranteed or fixed returns. In crypto, guaranteed returns are a hallmark of fraud.
