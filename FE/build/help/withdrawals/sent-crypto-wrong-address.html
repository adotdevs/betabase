I sent crypto to the wrong address

Almost always, the funds are gone. Blockchain transactions are irreversible — once confirmed, a transfer cannot be cancelled, reversed or recalled by you, by Betabase, or by anyone.

The honest answer

There is no "undo" on a blockchain and no central authority who can claw a payment back. That is not a Betabase policy — it is how the technology works, and it is the trade-off that makes crypto censorship-resistant in the first place.

The only realistic situations where anything can be done:

  • You sent to another customer of Betabase. We may be able to contact them, but we cannot force a return. They have to agree.
  • You sent to another exchange. That exchange controls the address. Contact them directly with the TXID. Whether they act is entirely their decision.
  • You sent to a wallet a person you know controls. Ask them to send it back.
  • You sent to an invalid or unowned address. Nobody holds the key. It is unrecoverable, permanently.

Steps

  1. Find the transaction hash (TXID) in Transactions.
  2. Check it on a block explorer to confirm where it actually went.
  3. If the address belongs to an exchange, open a ticket with that exchange, quoting the TXID, asset, network, amount and date.
  4. If you believe you were defrauded, report it to your local police or cybercrime unit and give them the TXID.

How to never do this again

  • Whitelist your addresses.
  • Always send a small test amount first.
  • Copy addresses with the copy button, then check the first and last four characters — clipboard-hijacking malware swaps addresses silently.
  • Scan the QR code where possible.

Anyone promising to "recover" your lost crypto for an upfront fee is running a second scam on a victim of the first. There is no such service. Betabase will never ask for your password, 2FA code, seed phrase or private keys.
