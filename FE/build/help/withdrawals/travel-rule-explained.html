Travel Rule: why we ask who owns the receiving wallet

The Travel Rule is a global anti-money-laundering regulation requiring us to collect and pass on information about who is sending and who is receiving a crypto transfer.

What it means in practice

When you withdraw crypto, Betabase must ask:

  • Is the receiving wallet yours, or someone else's?
  • If it belongs to another exchange or provider, which one.
  • If it belongs to another person, their full name and — depending on the amount and country — their address or date of birth.
  • If it is your own self-hosted wallet, we may ask you to prove you control it.

Where the destination is another regulated provider, the required originator and beneficiary details "travel" with the transaction to them, securely. This mirrors the rule that has applied to bank transfers for decades.

Good to know

  • The rule comes from international standards (FATF Recommendation 16) and is written into law in most jurisdictions we operate in. It is not optional for us, and there is no version of Betabase where you can opt out.
  • Thresholds vary by country. Small transfers may need less information than large ones.
  • Information is shared only with the receiving regulated provider and, where legally required, with authorities. It is not published, sold, or written to the blockchain.
  • Answering inaccurately breaches the Terms of Service and can freeze the account.
  • If you decline to provide the information, the withdrawal cannot be released. Your funds stay in your account — they are not confiscated.

Proving you own a wallet never requires your seed phrase. Betabase will never ask for your password, 2FA code, seed phrase or private keys. Any "wallet verification" tool that asks for your recovery words is stealing your funds, and blockchain transactions are irreversible.
