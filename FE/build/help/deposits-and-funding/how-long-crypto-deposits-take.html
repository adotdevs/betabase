How long do crypto deposits take? (confirmations explained)

A crypto deposit credits once it has enough confirmations on its network — usually minutes, occasionally an hour or more when the network is busy.

What a confirmation is

When you send crypto, the transaction waits in a queue (the mempool) until a validator packs it into a block. That is confirmation one. Every block built on top of it adds another confirmation, making the transaction progressively harder to undo. Betabase waits for a set number of confirmations per asset before crediting your balance — a slow, heavy network needs fewer; a fast, light one needs more.

Steps to track a pending deposit

  1. Open Transactions in Betabase. The deposit appears with a Pending status and a confirmation count.
  2. Copy the transaction hash (TXID) from your sending wallet.
  3. Paste it into a public block explorer for that network.
  4. Read the status:

- Not found — the transfer never left your wallet. The problem is on the sending side. - Pending / unconfirmed — it is in the queue. Wait. A low network fee means a longer wait. - Confirmed — it is on-chain, and Betabase will credit it once the required confirmation count is reached.

Good to know

  • Crypto networks run 24/7 — weekends and holidays make no difference.
  • A transaction sent with too low a network fee can sit unconfirmed for hours. Only the sender's wallet can attempt to speed it up (via RBF or a child-pays-for-parent transaction), never Betabase.
  • Confirmed on-chain but not credited after several hours? Contact support@www.betabase.pro with the TXID, asset, network and amount. Support is available 24/7.

Blockchain transactions are irreversible. Once broadcast, a transfer cannot be cancelled or recalled by you or by Betabase.
