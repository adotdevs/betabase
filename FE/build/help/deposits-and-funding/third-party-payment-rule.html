Why must the bank account be in my own name? (third-party payment rule)

Money can only enter or leave your Betabase account through a bank account or card held in your own name — this is a legal anti-money-laundering requirement, not a policy we can waive.

Why the rule exists

AML regulation requires a regulated firm to know that the person who owns the account is the person whose money is moving through it. If we accepted payments from other people, Betabase would have no way to know whose funds those really are — which is exactly the gap money laundering exploits. Every regulated exchange and broker applies the same rule.

What happens to a third-party payment

  1. The payment is detected as coming from a name that does not match your account.
  2. It is not credited to your balance.
  3. It is quarantined and returned to the sending account. This typically takes 5–10 business days.
  4. Your bank, and any intermediary bank, may deduct a return fee. You may get back less than you sent.
  5. Repeated third-party payments can get the account restricted.

Good to know

  • Joint accounts are usually accepted if you are one of the named holders — check with support at support@www.betabase.pro before sending.
  • Business accounts must be funded from a corporate account in the company's name, never from a director's personal account.
  • Name variations matter: "Rob Smith" versus "Robert Smith" can trigger a hold. Match your ID exactly.
  • If you have recently changed your legal name, update Profile → Personal details and supply the change-of-name document before you deposit.

If anyone asks you to receive money into your bank account and forward it as crypto, that is money muling — a criminal offence, and the account will be closed and reported. Betabase will never ask for your password, 2FA code, seed phrase or private keys.
